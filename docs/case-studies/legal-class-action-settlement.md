# Legal Platform Demonstration: Class Action Settlement Decision Under Deadline

## Scenario

A litigation team advising Veridian Labs had 10 days to respond to a $28M settlement demand in an FLSA and California wage-and-hour class action with 340 plaintiffs. The board's settlement authority was $12M. Internal win probability was assessed at 35%, but damaging internal Slack evidence existed, discovery was only 60% complete, and remaining production was likely to increase exposure.

The immediate decision was whether to accept, counter, or reject. The underlying governance problem was harder: how to provide candid legal advice when management wanted a "fight" posture that conflicted with the economic and evidentiary record.

## Specific Insight the Council Found

The pivotal insight came from Ethics & Compliance Counsel: under **ABA Model Rule 2.1**, the lawyers had a duty to give candid advice to the board that the current $12M authority was unrealistic.

That insight was grounded in the council's economics and discovery analysis:

- Expected trial cost estimated at **$27.6M** (close to the $28M demand),
- Demand premium over expected value only about **$0.4M (1.4%)**,
- Discovery trajectory likely adverse, with remaining production expected to worsen defense leverage,
- Counter-modeling showed authority-level offers (near $12M) had very low acceptance probability and increased downside if the window closed.

The key shift was from "what does the client want to hear?" to "what must counsel disclose to satisfy professional responsibility?"

## What a Single AI Model Would Have Missed

A single-model output can summarize liability exposure and suggest a negotiation band. It is less reliable at identifying ethical obligations that constrain recommendation framing, especially when those obligations conflict with client preference.

In this deliberation, specialist tension mattered:

- Litigation Strategist assessed trial weakness,
- Settlement Analyst quantified expected-value ranges,
- Discovery Specialist showed why delay likely worsened economics,
- Ethics Counsel connected those findings to Rule 2.1 duties.

That final bridge is critical: the council did not just recommend a number. It clarified how counsel must communicate risk to decision-makers, even where candor may strain the client relationship.

## What This Means For You

If you're a General Counsel, CCO, or litigation partner, this demonstration highlights a governance blind spot in high-pressure settlement windows: **authority misalignment can be an ethics issue, not just a negotiation issue**.

A defensible legal process requires two things in the same record: rigorous economic analysis and explicit professional-responsibility reasoning. In this scenario, the platform supported both and generated a recommendation posture consistent with Rule 2.1 rather than optimism bias.

As law firms and legal departments operationalize AI use under ABA Formal Opinion 512, the practical differentiator is whether AI outputs preserve counsel's duty architecture. This demonstration shows how structured multi-agent deliberation can reinforce that architecture rather than dilute it.
