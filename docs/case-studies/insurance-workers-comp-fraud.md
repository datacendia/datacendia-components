# Insurance Platform Demonstration: Workers' Comp Fraud vs. Legitimate Injury

## Scenario

Keystone Mutual (regional workers' compensation carrier) reviewed a $1.4M reserved claim involving an MRI-confirmed L4-L5 herniation. The file had a fraud score of 74/100 (above SIU referral threshold), with suspicious attorney/provider patterns: 47 related claims in 18 months and concentration around three providers.

The decision was not binary. Deny too aggressively and risk bad-faith exposure; pay blindly and potentially fund organized inflation. The challenge was distinguishing legitimate injury value from potentially inflated treatment pathways.

## Specific Insight the Council Found

The pivotal insight came from the Chief Actuary's reserve decomposition. Even assuming the injury was legitimate, the expected cost profile did not support a $1.4M reserve if treatment was redirected through independent medical review.

The council isolated a defensible scenario value of **$416K** for a legitimate claim path (indemnity plus medically justified treatment), then quantified the delta:

- Current reserve: **$1.4M**
- Legitimate claim value: **$416K**
- Implied inflation component: **$984K**

That changed the frame from "fraudulent vs. real claim" to "real injury plus probable provider-ring inflation."

The ring-level implication was equally important: if similar inflation patterns applied across linked files, exposure moved from a single-claim decision to a portfolio problem measured in tens of millions.

## What a Single AI Model Would Have Missed

A single-model decision often collapses this kind of case into one of two errors: over-trusting fraud flags (leading to bad-faith risk) or over-correcting toward payment (missing systemic abuse).

The council's adversarial structure prevented both by forcing specialists to test separate hypotheses:

- Claims Director validated objective injury facts,
- Fraud Investigator localized strongest signal to attorney-provider concentration,
- Chief Actuary quantified inflation economics,
- Compliance Officer modeled unfair-claims and bad-faith downside if denial was wrong.

This produced a more actionable path: approve legitimate claim elements, require IME for high-cost intervention decisions, and escalate mandatory fraud reporting and ring-level investigation.

## What This Means For You

If you're a Chief Actuary, SIU lead, or VP Claims, the lesson is practical: **reserve governance should explicitly separate injury validity from treatment-path inflation**.

In this demonstration, the value was not a flashy "deny or pay" answer. It was a decision packet that preserved claimant fairness, reduced unnecessary spend, and aligned with reporting obligations under state fraud frameworks and NAIC-aligned principles.

For carriers facing tighter AI governance scrutiny from state insurance departments, this kind of decomposition matters. It creates a defensible record that explains why the claim was handled as legitimate while still pursuing potential organized fraud where evidence is strongest.
