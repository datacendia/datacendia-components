# Real Estate Platform Demonstration: Office-to-Residential Conversion Credit Decision

## Scenario

Harbor National Bank evaluated a $70.5M construction loan request for a $94M office-to-residential conversion in downtown Portland. The asset was a 22-story Class B office tower with 76% vacancy, acquired at foreclosure for $31M, with a proposal to convert to 340 residential units.

The developer projected a stabilized value of $136M and attractive leverage metrics. But zoning approval was still pending, asbestos abatement was not fully scoped, and independent construction estimates were higher than the sponsor's assumptions. This is a classic moment where underwriting discipline determines whether a "counter-cyclical opportunity" becomes a durable asset or a trapped capital position.

## Specific Insight the Council Found

The pivotal insight came from the Chief Appraiser's valuation challenge. Using adjusted assumptions for achievable rents, stabilized vacancy, and a conversion-appropriate cap rate, the appraiser recalculated stabilized value at **$106M instead of $136M**.

That is a **$30M reduction** in value from the sponsor's model.

The council showed where the gap came from:

- Rent assumption moved from $2,180/month to $1,950/month,
- Stabilized vacancy from 5% to 7%,
- Cap rate from 4.0% to 4.5% to reflect conversion risk premium.

The resulting underwriting frame was materially different: the same loan request could still be financeable, but only with tighter structure, stronger contingencies, and milestone-based risk controls.

## What a Single AI Model Would Have Missed

A single-model analysis could produce a polished memo, but it often fails to force contradictory assumptions into explicit financial consequences. In this deliberation, specialist cross-examination exposed where the original model was optimistic rather than bankable.

- Underwriting Manager highlighted budget stressors (construction gap, unscoped asbestos, permit dependency),
- Chief Appraiser reset valuation assumptions to lending-grade inputs,
- Market Analyst stress-tested supply pipeline risk (conversion pipeline approaching 80% of annual absorption),
- Compliance Officer validated low direct fair-lending friction while flagging reputational considerations.

The critical outcome was not simply "approve" or "reject." It was a restructured risk posture: underwrite to independent value, not aspirational exit value, and treat zoning as an existential precondition.

## What This Means For You

If you're a CCO, CRE lender, or credit committee lead, this demonstration reinforces a core principle: **conversion deals fail when pro forma optimism is mistaken for collateral value.**

The practical value of council-style deliberation is that it compresses valuation, market, and structure debates into one auditable decision packet before commitment. Here, the bank could still pursue the transaction, but from a materially safer position anchored to a $106M underwriting value and explicit risk controls.

For institutions navigating scrutiny on commercial real estate concentration and disciplined credit administration, this approach provides a defensible narrative for why terms changed, why conditions were imposed, and how downside was quantified before capital was deployed.
