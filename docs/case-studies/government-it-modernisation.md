# Government Platform Demonstration: FAR Part 15 Source Selection

## Scenario

A federal agency needed to finalize source selection for a $47M IT modernization contract under FAR Part 15. Three offerors were in play: Offeror A at $47.2M (Outstanding technical), incumbent Offeror B at $41.8M (Good technical), and Offeror C at $52.9M.

At first glance, the incumbent looked like the prudent fiscal choice: lowest evaluated price and low transition risk. But technical factors were explicitly more important than price in the solicitation, FedRAMP status carried execution risk, and the losing bidder was likely to file a GAO protest if tradeoff documentation was weak.

## Specific Insight the Council Found

The pivotal insight came from the Program Manager's total cost of ownership (TCO) analysis. The incumbent's lower bid depended on retaining COBOL-heavy legacy architecture with a modernization layer. When post-contract lifecycle costs were modeled, the apparent savings reversed:

- Offeror A 10-year total (contract + post-contract): **$55.7M**
- Offeror B 10-year total (contract + post-contract): **$77.8M**

The "cheaper" incumbent was projected to cost **$22.1M more over 10 years**.

This reframing gave the source selection authority a defensible value narrative aligned to mission continuity and long-term stewardship, not just upfront contract pricing.

## What a Single AI Model Would Have Missed

A single-model recommendation can compare scorecards, cite FAR language, and produce generic tradeoff text. What it often misses is the interplay between legal protest resilience and technical debt economics.

Here, the council surfaced both dimensions at once:

- Legal Counsel identified three GAO protest vulnerabilities (conclusory tradeoff rationale, treatment of past-performance differential, and FedRAMP-risk handling).
- Procurement Officer translated those into specific SSDD language patterns and documentation tasks.
- Program Manager quantified lifecycle debt from COBOL retention.
- Budget Analyst validated that TCO logic was fiscally coherent despite the higher award price.

The result was a stronger recommendation: select Offeror A, but only after closing explicit documentation gaps that would otherwise make the decision vulnerable on protest review.

## What This Means For You

If you're a Program Manager, Contracting Officer, or acquisition lead, this demonstration shows a practical point: **best value is not lowest price when legacy architecture debt is left unpriced.**

It also shows how governance can be operationalized under FAR Part 15: require each recommendation to include both narrative discriminators and auditable financial assumptions. That combination strengthens both mission delivery and legal defensibility.

For teams implementing OMB AI governance expectations and facing increasing scrutiny of acquisition rationale, this style of deliberation produces a decision packet that can survive internal review, external protest pressure, and later oversight without rewriting the record.
