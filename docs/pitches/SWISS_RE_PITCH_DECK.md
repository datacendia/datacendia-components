## Slide 2 — Swiss Re's Challenge

### Underwriting Decisions Under Increasing Scrutiny

- Reinsurance decisions involve billions in exposure
- Climate change, pandemic risk, and geopolitical instability create novel risks
- Regulators and cedents ask: *How do you justify risk acceptance?*

**The scrutiny vectors:**

1. **Regulators (FINMA, PRA, state DOIs)** — Capital adequacy, risk selection
2. **Cedents** — Why was this risk priced this way?
3. **Shareholders** — What governance exists for catastrophic risk decisions?
4. **Litigation** — Post-loss scrutiny of underwriting judgment

---

**"In reinsurance, the decision you made in 2026 will be questioned in 2030 — after the loss."**

---

## Slide 7 — Swiss Re Scenario

### Example: Catastrophe Treaty Renewal with Climate Considerations

**Scenario:**

Major property cat treaty renewal. Cedent in region with increasing climate exposure.
- Model suggests rate increase
- Cedent relationship pressure to hold pricing
- Climate models show uncertainty range

**The governance question:**
- Why was this rate accepted?
- How was climate uncertainty factored?
- Who approved deviation from technical price?
- Is there defensible evidence?

**With Datacendia:**

1. Technical price captured with model version
2. Climate scenario analysis documented
3. Relationship considerations recorded (with limits)
4. Underwriter judgment captured: rationale for final price
5. Approval chain documented
6. Immutable artifact created before binding

## Slide 9 — Why This Matters for Reinsurance

### The Long-Tail Nature of Scrutiny

- Reinsurance decisions face scrutiny years after binding
- By then, the underwriter may have moved on
- Institutional memory fades; evidence doesn't

**The asymmetry:**

- Good decision with bad outcome: defensible if documented
- Good decision with no documentation: indefensible

---

**"In reinsurance, you're not just making decisions for today. You're creating evidence for the future."**

# Objection Handling

## "Our underwriting workflow already captures rationale."

> "It captures the outcome. But can you prove, five years from now, exactly how climate considerations were weighed, why the deviation from technical price was justified, and who approved it? That's what post-loss scrutiny demands."

## "This seems like it could slow down quoting."

> "The deliberation capture takes minutes, not hours. It runs parallel to your existing workflow. The alternative — having no defensible evidence when a $500M loss is questioned — is far more costly."

## "How does this integrate with our underwriting systems?"

> "API integration with your workflow systems, pricing tools, and accumulation platforms. Datacendia captures the human judgment layer; your systems handle the technical work."
