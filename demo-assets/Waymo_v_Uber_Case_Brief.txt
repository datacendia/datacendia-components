CASE BRIEF: Waymo LLC v. Uber Technologies, Inc.
================================================================================

Court: U.S. District Court, Northern District of California
Case No.: 3:17-cv-00939
Filed: February 23, 2017

================================================================================
PARTIES
================================================================================

Plaintiff: Waymo LLC (Alphabet/Google subsidiary)
Defendant: Uber Technologies, Inc.; Otto Trucking LLC; Anthony Levandowski

================================================================================
FACTUAL BACKGROUND
================================================================================

Anthony Levandowski was a lead engineer at Google's self-driving car project 
(now Waymo) from 2009-2016. In January 2016, Levandowski downloaded approximately 
14,000 files (9.7 GB) from Google's design server, including proprietary LiDAR 
designs. He resigned from Google on January 27, 2016, and founded Otto, a 
self-driving truck company, which Uber acquired for $680 million in August 2016.

Timeline of Key Events:
- 2009: Levandowski joins Google's self-driving car project
- December 2015: Levandowski begins discussions with Uber executives
- January 14, 2016: Downloads 14,000 files to personal laptop
- January 27, 2016: Resigns from Google
- January 2016: Founds Otto with other former Google engineers
- August 2016: Uber acquires Otto for $680 million
- February 2017: Waymo files lawsuit

================================================================================
CLAIMS
================================================================================

1. Misappropriation of trade secrets (federal DTSA and California CUTSA)
2. Patent infringement (U.S. Patent Nos. 8,836,922 and 9,368,936)
3. Unfair competition (California Business & Professions Code § 17200)
4. Breach of contract (confidentiality and invention assignment agreements)

================================================================================
KEY EVIDENCE
================================================================================

For Waymo:
- Server logs showing 14,000 file downloads to personal laptop
- Levandowski's resignation timing (days after download)
- Uber's acquisition of Otto within 7 months of Levandowski's departure
- Technical similarities between Waymo and Uber LiDAR designs
- Levandowski invoked 5th Amendment 423 times during deposition
- Email evidence of Uber executives' knowledge of Google technology
- Forensic evidence of file transfers and deletions

For Uber:
- Independent development documentation
- Uber's own LiDAR research predating Otto acquisition
- Lack of direct evidence files were used at Uber
- Levandowski's personal expertise vs. trade secrets

================================================================================
TRADE SECRET ELEMENTS (California CUTSA)
================================================================================

To prevail, Waymo must prove:
1. Information qualifies as a trade secret
2. Waymo took reasonable measures to protect secrecy
3. Defendant acquired, disclosed, or used the trade secret
4. Defendant knew or should have known of misappropriation

================================================================================
PRIOR RELEVANT CASES
================================================================================

1. Silvaco Data Systems v. Intel Corp. (2010)
   - California trade secret elements
   - Requirement of "use" for misappropriation

2. DVD Copy Control Ass'n v. Bunner (2003)
   - California trade secret injunction standards
   - First Amendment considerations

3. Defend Trade Secrets Act of 2016
   - Federal cause of action for trade secret misappropriation
   - Ex parte seizure provisions
   - Whistleblower immunity provisions

4. Kewanee Oil Co. v. Bicron Corp. (1974)
   - Trade secret protection does not preempt patent law
   - Reasonable secrecy measures standard

================================================================================
DAMAGES ANALYSIS
================================================================================

Waymo's Claimed Damages: $1.86 billion

Calculation Methods:
1. Unjust Enrichment: Value of R&D Uber avoided ($500M-$1B)
2. Reasonable Royalty: What Uber would have paid for license
3. Lost Profits: Waymo's competitive disadvantage
4. Head Start: Time advantage gained by Uber (estimated 2-3 years)

Uber's Counter-Arguments:
- No evidence files were actually used
- Uber had independent LiDAR development
- Speculative damages calculation
- No actual lost sales by Waymo

================================================================================
SETTLEMENT OUTCOME
================================================================================

Date: February 9, 2018 (5 days into trial)
Terms: Uber paid Waymo $245 million in Uber equity (0.34% of company)
Additional: Uber agreed not to use Waymo trade secrets

Why Settlement?
- Uber facing damaging testimony from former executives
- Criminal investigation of Levandowski ongoing
- Uber's IPO timeline at risk
- Waymo achieved injunctive relief and validation

================================================================================
KEY LEGAL QUESTIONS FOR ANALYSIS
================================================================================

1. Did Uber have actual or constructive knowledge of misappropriation?
2. Were the LiDAR designs actually "used" by Uber, or merely possessed?
3. What is the appropriate damages measure for trade secret cases?
4. How does the 5th Amendment invocation affect civil liability?
5. Should Waymo have pursued trial for larger damages?
