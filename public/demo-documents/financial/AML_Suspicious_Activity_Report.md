## 4. NARRATIVE

### 4.1 Summary of Suspicious Activity

First National Bank of Commerce ("the Bank") is filing this Suspicious Activity Report to report unusual transaction patterns involving [SUBJECT 1] and the associated business entity Global Trade Solutions LLC ("GTS").

### 4.2 Account History and Relationship

[SUBJECT 1] opened a personal checking account in March 2019 with an initial deposit of $5,000. The account showed normal activity consistent with a small business owner until October 2025. GTS maintains a business checking account opened in June 2020, which historically processed $50,000-$75,000 monthly in legitimate-appearing trade transactions.

### 4.3 Description of Suspicious Activity

Beginning October 1, 2025, the Bank's automated transaction monitoring system flagged the following anomalous patterns:

**Pattern 1: Structured Cash Deposits**
Between October 1 and December 31, 2025, [SUBJECT 1] made 97 cash deposits to the personal account, all between $8,500 and $9,900. The deposits occurred at multiple branch locations throughout the Miami metropolitan area, often multiple times per day. Total structured deposits: $847,000.

| Month | Deposits | Total Amount | Avg. Deposit |
|-------|----------|--------------|--------------|
| October | 28 | $254,800 | $9,100 |
| November | 34 | $301,200 | $8,859 |
| December | 35 | $291,000 | $8,314 |

**Pattern 2: Rapid Fund Movement**
Within 24-48 hours of cash deposits, funds were transferred from the personal account to the GTS business account. The funds were then wire transferred to accounts at foreign financial institutions, primarily in:
- Panama (Banco Nacional de Panama): $650,000
- United Arab Emirates (Emirates NBD): $550,000
- Hong Kong (HSBC Hong Kong): $450,000

**Pattern 3: Inconsistent Business Activity**
GTS purports to be an import/export business dealing in consumer electronics. However:
- No corresponding trade documentation was provided for wire transfers
- Shipping records do not match transaction volumes
- Counterparties are shell companies with no verifiable business operations
- Wire transfer memos contain vague descriptions ("consulting services," "trade settlement")

**Pattern 4: Unusual Inbound Wires**
The account received $350,500 in inbound wires from entities in jurisdictions with elevated money laundering risk:
- Cyprus: $150,000 (3 wires)
- Malta: $125,500 (2 wires)
- British Virgin Islands: $75,000 (1 wire)

### 4.5 Customer Contact

On December 15, 2025, the Bank's BSA Officer contacted [SUBJECT 1] to request documentation supporting recent business transactions. The customer:
- Initially agreed to provide invoices and shipping documents
- Failed to provide documentation within the requested timeframe
- Became evasive when asked about the source of cash deposits
- Stated cash came from "various business activities" without specifics

### 4.6 Enhanced Due Diligence Findings

The Bank conducted enhanced due diligence which revealed:
- GTS has no employees other than [SUBJECT 1]
- Business address is a virtual office/mail drop
- No physical inventory or warehouse facilities identified
- Delaware corporate records show minimal filing history
- Beneficial ownership information may be incomplete
