# CASE BRIEF: Waymo LLC v. Uber Technologies, Inc.

**Court:** U.S. District Court, Northern District of California  
**Case No.:** 3:17-cv-00939  
**Judge:** Hon. William Alsup  
**Filed:** February 23, 2017  
**Resolved:** February 9, 2018 (Settlement)

---

## PARTIES

**Plaintiff:** Waymo LLC  
- Subsidiary of Alphabet Inc. (Google's parent company)
- Develops autonomous vehicle technology
- Formerly known as Google Self-Driving Car Project

**Defendants:**
- Uber Technologies, Inc.
- Otto Trucking LLC (acquired by Uber)
- Anthony Levandowski (individual defendant)

---

## FACTUAL BACKGROUND

### The Technology
Waymo's LiDAR (Light Detection and Ranging) system is critical to autonomous vehicle navigation. The technology uses laser pulses to create 3D maps of the vehicle's surroundings. Waymo invested over $1 billion developing proprietary LiDAR designs that were significantly smaller, cheaper, and more accurate than competitors.

### The Alleged Theft
1. **December 2015:** Anthony Levandowski, a senior engineer at Google's self-driving car project, accessed Google's design server and downloaded approximately 14,000 files (9.7 GB) containing proprietary LiDAR designs, supplier lists, and technical specifications.

2. **January 2016:** Levandowski transferred files to a personal laptop and wiped the laptop's contents using specialized software.

3. **January 27, 2016:** Levandowski resigned from Google.

4. **January 2016:** Levandowski founded Otto, a self-driving truck startup.

5. **August 2016:** Uber acquired Otto for approximately $680 million, with Levandowski becoming head of Uber's self-driving car division.

6. **December 2016:** A Waymo supplier accidentally copied Waymo on an email containing technical drawings that appeared to be Waymo's proprietary LiDAR designs, but were labeled as Uber's.

---

## CLAIMS ASSERTED

### Count I: Trade Secret Misappropriation (Federal)
Under the Defend Trade Secrets Act of 2016 (18 U.S.C. § 1836)
- Alleged misappropriation of LiDAR designs, manufacturing processes, and supplier information

### Count II: Trade Secret Misappropriation (State)
Under California Uniform Trade Secrets Act (Cal. Civ. Code § 3426)

### Count III: Patent Infringement
- U.S. Patent No. 8,836,922 (LiDAR system)
- U.S. Patent No. 9,368,936 (Autonomous vehicle sensors)

### Count IV: Unfair Competition
Under California Business & Professions Code § 17200

### Count V: Breach of Contract
Levandowski's confidentiality and invention assignment agreements with Google

---

## KEY EVIDENCE

### For Waymo:
1. **Server logs** documenting 14,000 file downloads to Levandowski's personal laptop
2. **Timing:** Downloads occurred weeks before resignation
3. **Data wiping:** Evidence of specialized software used to erase laptop
4. **Technical similarities:** Expert testimony on design overlaps between Waymo and Uber LiDAR
5. **The "smoking gun" email:** Supplier accidentally revealed Uber designs matching Waymo's
6. **Fifth Amendment:** Levandowski invoked his right against self-incrimination 423 times during deposition

### For Uber:
1. **Independent development:** Uber claimed its LiDAR was developed independently
2. **No file access:** Uber claimed it never received or used the downloaded files
3. **Due diligence:** Uber claimed it conducted appropriate due diligence before Otto acquisition

## PRIOR RELEVANT CASES

### Trade Secret Elements
- **Silvaco Data Systems v. Intel Corp.**, 184 Cal. App. 4th 210 (2010)
  - Defines trade secret misappropriation elements under California law
  
- **DVD Copy Control Ass'n v. Bunner**, 116 Cal. App. 4th 241 (2004)
  - Trade secret protection requirements

### Employee Departure Cases
- **Brocade Communications v. A10 Networks**, 873 F. Supp. 2d 1192 (N.D. Cal. 2012)
  - Similar fact pattern of departing employees and alleged trade secret theft

### Damages Methodology
- **Lucent Technologies v. Gateway**, 580 F.3d 1301 (Fed. Cir. 2009)
  - Reasonable royalty calculation methodology

## DAMAGES CLAIMED

**$1.86 billion** based on:
- Value of stolen technology
- Uber's avoided R&D costs
- Waymo's competitive harm
- Unjust enrichment

---

## PROCEDURAL HISTORY

1. **Feb 2017:** Complaint filed
2. **Mar 2017:** Preliminary injunction motion filed
3. **May 2017:** Court orders Levandowski removed from Uber's LiDAR work
4. **Aug 2017:** Levandowski fired by Uber for refusing to cooperate
5. **Oct 2017:** Trial date set for February 2018
6. **Feb 5, 2018:** Trial begins, jury seated
7. **Feb 9, 2018:** Settlement announced after 4 days of trial

---

## SETTLEMENT TERMS

- Uber paid Waymo **$245 million in Uber equity** (0.34% of company)
- Uber agreed to ensure its LiDAR technology does not incorporate Waymo trade secrets
- Independent technical expert to verify compliance
- No admission of liability

---

## KEY LEGAL ISSUES FOR ANALYSIS

1. **Trade Secret Identification:** Were Waymo's LiDAR designs sufficiently identified as trade secrets?

2. **Misappropriation:** Did Uber "acquire" the trade secrets through improper means, or merely hire an employee who happened to have knowledge?

3. **Inevitable Disclosure:** Should Levandowski have been barred from working on LiDAR at Uber under the inevitable disclosure doctrine?

4. **Corporate Liability:** Is Uber liable for Levandowski's pre-employment conduct?

5. **Damages:** How should damages be calculated when the technology was allegedly never used?
