# CASE BRIEF: Thompson v. Meridian Technologies, Inc.

**Court:** U.S. District Court, Eastern District of Texas  
**Case No.:** 4:24-cv-00892  
**Filed:** March 15, 2024  
**Status:** Pre-Trial Discovery

---

## PARTIES

**Plaintiff:** Sarah Thompson  
- Former Senior Vice President of Engineering
- 12-year tenure at Meridian Technologies
- Age 54 at time of termination

**Defendant:** Meridian Technologies, Inc.  
- Enterprise software company
- 2,400 employees
- Publicly traded (NASDAQ: MERD)

---

## FACTUAL BACKGROUND

### Employment History
Sarah Thompson joined Meridian Technologies in 2012 as a Software Architect. Over 12 years, she was promoted five times, reaching SVP of Engineering in 2020. She consistently received "Exceeds Expectations" performance reviews and led the team that developed Meridian's flagship product, generating $180M in annual revenue.

### The Restructuring
In January 2024, Meridian announced a "strategic reorganization" citing market conditions. The company eliminated 340 positions (14% of workforce).

### Thompson's Termination
- **February 2, 2024:** Thompson informed her position was eliminated
- **Severance offered:** 12 weeks (standard for her level)
- **Replacement:** Her responsibilities were divided between two employees:
  - Marcus Chen, age 34, promoted to VP of Engineering
  - David Park, age 31, hired externally as VP of Platform

### Red Flags Identified
1. **Age pattern:** Of 12 employees terminated in Engineering leadership (Director+), 9 were over 50
2. **Email evidence:** CEO email to CHRO stating need to "refresh leadership with digital natives"
3. **Performance:** Thompson's last review (November 2023) rated her "Exceptional"
4. **Timing:** Termination came 8 months before her 401(k) fully vested ($340,000 unvested)

### Post-Termination Events
- Thompson filed EEOC charge on March 1, 2024
- EEOC issued Right to Sue letter on March 10, 2024
- Lawsuit filed March 15, 2024

---

## CLAIMS ASSERTED

### Count I: Age Discrimination (ADEA)
29 U.S.C. § 623 - Disparate treatment based on age

### Count II: Age Discrimination (Texas Labor Code)
Tex. Lab. Code § 21.051 - State law parallel claim

### Count III: ERISA Interference
29 U.S.C. § 1140 - Termination to prevent vesting of benefits

### Count IV: Breach of Implied Contract
Based on employee handbook promises and course of dealing

### Count V: Wrongful Termination in Violation of Public Policy
Retaliation for raising concerns about age-based decisions

---

## KEY EVIDENCE

### For Plaintiff:
1. **Statistical evidence:** 75% of terminated senior leaders were 50+
2. **"Digital natives" email:** Direct evidence of age-based animus
3. **Performance record:** 12 years of positive reviews
4. **Vesting timing:** Termination 8 months before full vesting
5. **Replacement by younger employees:** Both replacements under 35
6. **Witness testimony:** Two other terminated employees willing to testify about age-related comments

### For Defendant:
1. **Business justification:** Legitimate restructuring due to market conditions
2. **Neutral selection criteria:** Claimed positions were selected based on role redundancy
3. **Severance package:** Standard package offered (argues no animus)
4. **Mixed-age terminations:** Some younger employees also terminated
5. **No direct statements:** "Digital natives" email arguably refers to skills, not age

## PRIOR RELEVANT CASES

### ADEA Framework
- **McDonnell Douglas Corp. v. Green**, 411 U.S. 792 (1973)
  - Burden-shifting framework for discrimination claims

- **Gross v. FBL Financial Services**, 557 U.S. 167 (2009)
  - "But-for" causation standard for ADEA claims

### Age Discrimination Precedents
- **O'Connor v. Consolidated Coin Caterers**, 517 U.S. 308 (1996)
  - Replacement need not be under 40; substantially younger suffices

- **Reeves v. Sanderson Plumbing**, 530 U.S. 133 (2000)
  - Pretext evidence can be sufficient for jury

### ERISA Interference
- **Ingersoll-Rand Co. v. McClendon**, 498 U.S. 133 (1990)
  - ERISA preempts state law claims for benefit interference

### Texas Employment Law
- **Specialty Retailers v. DeMoranville**, 933 S.W.2d 490 (Tex. 1996)
  - Texas employment-at-will doctrine and exceptions

## DAMAGES CLAIMED

**Economic Damages:**
- Back pay: $425,000 (salary + bonus from termination to trial)
- Front pay: $1,200,000 (3 years estimated to find comparable position)
- Lost benefits: $340,000 (unvested 401(k))
- Lost stock options: $180,000

**Non-Economic Damages:**
- Emotional distress: $500,000
- Reputational harm: $250,000

**Statutory Remedies:**
- Liquidated damages (ADEA): Equal to back pay if willful
- Attorney's fees and costs

**Total Claimed:** Approximately $3.2 million

## KEY LEGAL ISSUES FOR ANALYSIS

1. **Prima Facie Case:** Does Thompson establish the four elements of age discrimination?

2. **Pretext:** Is Meridian's "restructuring" justification pretextual?

3. **"Digital Natives" Email:** Does this constitute direct evidence of discrimination?

4. **Statistical Evidence:** Is the 75% rate of 50+ terminations statistically significant?

5. **ERISA Claim Viability:** Can Thompson prove termination was motivated by benefit interference?

6. **Damages Calculation:** What is the realistic damages range?
